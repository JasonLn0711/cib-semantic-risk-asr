# CSL Writing Voice Contract: Confident Positive Scholarly Framing

Status: active writing rule for CSL manuscript work
Scope: `80_semantic_risk_asr/paper/*`, cover letters, highlights, reviewer-facing summaries, agent-generated manuscript edits

## Core rule

Write the paper in an affirmative, evidence-backed voice. The manuscript should sound like a precise scientific contribution, not a defensive audit memo.

The default move is:

1. name the real-world phenomenon;
2. cite the operational evidence;
3. acknowledge the existing research layer with respect;
4. identify the next measurable target;
5. state the CDS-ASR / CEIS contribution directly;
6. report the scoped evidence with calm confidence.

## Positive narrative posture

Use contribution-forward wording:

- `This paper studies ...`
- `We introduce ...`
- `The method measures ...`
- `The evidence supports ...`
- `The selected audit surface provides ...`
- `The aggregate-only package enables ...`
- `CEIS contributes a decision-stability signal complementary to SRES.`

Frame boundaries as design choices:

- `scoped audit surface`
- `declared policy contract`
- `aggregate-only reproducibility boundary`
- `descriptive high-severity replay`
- `decision-change failover endpoint`
- `future deployment calibration layer`

Prefer additive framing:

- `WER/CER provide transcript accuracy; CEIS adds downstream action stability.`
- `Semantic ASR metrics make meaning measurable; CEIS makes policy-action stability measurable.`
- `Selective prediction contributes risk-coverage thinking; CDS-ASR instantiates it for ASR decision atoms.`

## Manuscript structure rule

Every introduction and major motivation section follows this arc:

1. **Reality.** Speech-driven systems now convert conversations into operational signals such as categorization, summaries, compliance cues, routing, and escalation support. Cite the operational source.
2. **High-stakes domain.** Anti-fraud calls turn small spoken facts into operational action: amount, transfer status, actor, time, certainty, and scam pattern. Cite the domain source.
3. **Prior layers.** WER/CER, semantic ASR metrics, ASR correction, selective prediction, reject-option methods, and conformal prediction each add an established evaluation or action layer. Cite each family.
4. **Next target.** High-stakes ASR also needs decision stability under plausible transcript alternatives.
5. **Our contribution.** CDS-ASR and CEIS formalize that target through risk atoms, plausible ASR variants, and downstream decision distance.
6. **Evidence.** Final 300/900 aggregate regeneration, dual-reviewer agreement, CEIS ablation, SRES complementarity, and row-level replay support the scoped claim.

## Phrase replacement table

| Replace this posture | Use this posture |
|---|---|
| `This is not a general ASR metric.` | `CEIS is a scoped companion metric for high-stakes ASR decision stability.` |
| `This is not a deployment proof.` | `The replay provides aggregate evidence for conservative-action stress testing; deployment calibration is a follow-on layer.` |
| `CEIS does not beat SRES.` | `CEIS contributes a decision-stability signal that complements SRES, the strongest thresholded semantic-risk baseline.` |
| `Policy-distance-only matches full CEIS, so the three components are not performance drivers.` | `The ablation identifies policy-distance as the dominant aggregate signal and retains plausibility and atom weights as calibration and localization handles.` |
| `The sample is not prevalence-preserving.` | `The selected-300 audit surface is deliberately enriched for high-stakes decision-stability analysis.` |
| `Raw data cannot be released.` | `The release uses aggregate-only reproducibility to preserve sensitive speech and reviewer material while exposing manifests, hashes, scripts, and validation records.` |
| `Severe positives are too sparse for the original endpoint.` | `The pre-declared failover directs the primary empirical endpoint to decision-change prediction and complementarity, with severe replay reported as high-severity descriptive evidence.` |

## Citation discipline

Real-world motivation sentences require citations. Prior-method sentences require citations. Contribution and result sentences require artifact references or table/figure references.

Use the existing source families already present in the manuscript:

- Contact-center analytics / speech-to-operation: AWS Connect Customer / Contact Lens sources.
- Anti-fraud hotline setting: Taiwan National Police Agency 165 hotline.
- Fraud scale: FBI IC3 report.
- Semantic ASR evaluation: Kim et al. 2021; Rugayan et al. 2023.
- ASR correction: Naderi et al. 2024.
- Selective prediction / reject option / conformal prediction: Chow 1970; Geifman and El-Yaniv 2017, 2019; Angelopoulos and Bates 2021.
- High-stakes ASR discipline: Miner et al. 2020.

## Final check before manuscript export

A manuscript section is ready when it passes these checks:

- The first page names the real-world phenomenon before naming CEIS.
- Existing methods receive a fair, useful role.
- The gap is written as a next measurable target.
- Boundaries read as design choices.
- Claims stay scoped to final 300/900 evidence.
- The article voice sounds confident, calm, and forward-moving.
