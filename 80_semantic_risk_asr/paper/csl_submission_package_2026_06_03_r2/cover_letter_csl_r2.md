Dear Editors,

We submit "Counterfactual Decision Stability for High-Stakes Mandarin ASR:
Evaluating Safety-Relevant Instability Beyond Transcript Accuracy" for
consideration in Computer Speech & Language.

This submission introduces CEIS as a counterfactual decision-stability layer for
high-stakes Mandarin ASR. After final 300/900 regeneration, sparse severe
positives triggered the pre-declared endpoint failover: CEIS is evaluated as a
decision-change and complementarity signal rather than a total-superiority claim
over SRES, while severe missed-escalation replay is reported as descriptive
high-severity evidence.

The paper's contribution is an ASR evaluation layer that asks whether plausible
transcript alternatives around decision-bearing atoms would change a declared
downstream triage action. The work is scoped to a selected high-risk Mandarin
anti-fraud audit surface and uses completed dual-reviewer labels, final
aggregate predictor tables, CEIS ablations, SRES/CEIS complementarity checks,
and row-level conservative-action replay as evidence.

The artifact release follows an aggregate-only reproducibility boundary. Raw
audio, raw transcripts, row identifiers, model hypotheses, reviewer sheets,
reviewer notes, runtime logs, and model weights remain local-only because they
may contain sensitive call or review content. Reviewer-visible aggregate
artifacts, manifests, validation records, scripts, and release-candidate freeze
reports are included for auditability.
