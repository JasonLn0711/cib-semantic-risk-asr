\section{Introduction}

Sensitive speech AI research sits between two necessary commitments: empirical claims should be traceable, and transcript-bearing evidence should remain governed when it contains private operational speech content. Raw audio, transcripts, row identifiers, reviewer notes, response sheets, model hypotheses, and transcript-bearing runtime logs can carry contextual details that are subject to institutional, contractual, ethical, or safety controls. Reviewers still need to inspect whether the paper's claims follow from the evidence.

This paper makes reviewer auditability the central design target. Aggregate-only reproducibility exposes the evidence that can be safely shared---aggregate tables, figure inputs, artifact manifests, validation gates, operation records, claim registries, agreement summaries, and release-boundary decisions---while keeping transcript-bearing evidence inside the declared local governance boundary. The result is an inspectable evidence package rather than an opaque privacy caveat.

The paper uses the CDS-ASR project as a case study. The technical details of CDS-ASR and CEIS belong to the companion speech-evaluation manuscript. Here, CDS-ASR demonstrates how a sensitive speech AI study can turn model-comparison context, selected-300 provenance, completed dual-reviewer human audit evidence, agreement statistics, and aggregate policy replay into a reviewer-auditable package without public transcript release.
