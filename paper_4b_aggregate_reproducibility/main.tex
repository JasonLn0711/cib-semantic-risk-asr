\begin{abstract}
Sensitive speech AI studies increasingly produce evidence from audio, transcripts, model hypotheses, reviewer judgments, runtime logs, and operational decisions. Responsible AI documentation and risk-management practice encourage traceable evaluation, while sensitive speech settings require a release boundary that protects transcript-bearing material. This paper introduces aggregate-only reproducibility as a reviewer-auditable evidence architecture for that setting. The framework turns sensitive evaluation into a structured claim-evidence package: public aggregate artifacts, local-only transcript-bearing evidence, artifact manifests, validation gates, operation records, privacy classes, and explicit release-boundary decisions.

We develop the framework through a CDS-ASR case study in high-stakes speech-to-decision evaluation. The case contains four distinct evidence layers: a 258-row ASR test split for model-comparison context, selected-300 provenance outputs for enriched audit-surface construction, a completed 300-row dual-reviewer human audit with 900 model-level assessments per reviewer and Cohen's kappa agreement summaries, and aggregate policy replay for recovery analysis. Instead of releasing raw speech or transcript-bearing row content, the case provides reviewer-visible auditability through aggregate tables, generated figures, claim registries, artifact manifests, agreement summaries, validation checks, and release-boundary records.

The contribution is claim-evidence stewardship for sensitive speech AI. Aggregate-only reproducibility gives reviewers a concrete way to inspect artifact completeness, metric definitions, validation gates, agreement evidence, figure regeneration, and claim-scope alignment while transcript-bearing rows remain under governed local control. It complements row-level reproduction with a privacy-preserving audit mode for studies whose evidence can be made inspectable without public transcript release.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} trustworthy AI; reproducible evaluation; sensitive speech data; artifact manifest; claim-evidence alignment; responsible AI audit.
