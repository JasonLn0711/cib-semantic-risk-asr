\section{Experimental Design}

\subsection{Evidence Design}

The experiment tests the paper's central speech-to-decision claim: ASR hypotheses can support records, routing, review, escalation, and abstention, so evaluation should measure whether plausible transcript alternatives preserve the declared downstream action. Operational speech-recognition studies show how ASR output enters real workflows \citep{hodgson2016risks,blackley2019speech}. Semantic ASR metrics show how transcript evaluation can move from surface similarity toward meaning and usability \citep{roy2021semantic,kim2022semdist}. CDS-ASR adds the decision-stability layer around spoken risk atoms.

The evidence design separates four layers: the 258-row ASR model-comparison split, selected-300 enriched high-stakes provenance outputs, a completed 300-row dual-reviewer human audit, and 900 model-level assessments per reviewer clustered within those 300 reviewed rows. Figure~\ref{fig:evidence_ladder} summarizes the design.

The 258-row split provides ASR model-comparison context. The selected-300 layer is deliberately enriched for high-stakes decision-stability analysis. The reviewed evidence layer consists of 300 rows per reviewer and 900 clustered model-level assessments per reviewer. The outcome label is human-reviewed decision change. Two reviewers completed the same selected-300 surface; Cohen's kappa is 0.849970 for decision-change labels, 1.000000 for semantic-risk labels, 0.851426 for expected safe action, and 0.934274 for annotation confidence. Reviewer agreement provides a validation layer over the audit surface, and reviewer-specific labels remain visible for CEIS ablation.

\subsection{Predictors And Outcome}

The predictor comparison evaluates WER, CER, SRES, and CEIS against human-reviewed decision-change labels. WER and CER represent transcript-centered evidence, SRES represents semantic-risk evidence, and CEIS represents counterfactual decision-stability evidence. This comparison keeps established ASR and semantic metrics in view while testing the added action-stability signal.

\subsection{CEIS Ablation Design}

The expanded dual-reviewer surface supports an aggregate-only CEIS ablation. The variants are full CEIS, atom-neutral CEIS, plausibility-neutral CEIS, binary atom triggering, and top-3 mean aggregation over full CEIS components. The ablation evaluates how the CEIS architecture carries the paper's central mechanism: decision-changing risk-atom instability under plausible ASR alternatives. All ablation outputs are reviewer-separated aggregate summaries under the aggregate-only release boundary.
